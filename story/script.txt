In this town, every lie you tell, your body gets twenty pounds heavier. That's why honest people are skinny as broomsticks, and big liars can't fit through doors. Telling the truth takes it off. I was pretty average. But my best friend Benny was the most honest kid alive. Whenever someone opened a door, he blew down the hallway. Meanwhile, Chad was the biggest kid around. He said his uncle invented pizza. That he'd wrestled a shark. That he'd never lied. Coach only picked big kids. He asked if I'd played before. I said, tons. I got twenty pounds heavier. And that's when I had an idea. If I lied enough, I'd make the team. I told Mom I'd cleaned my room. My sister already had. Nothing happened. I told my teacher my dog ate my homework. My dog walked in chewing it. Still nothing. Then Benny showed me his drawing of me, and I said, I love it. It looked like a potato. So I started lying everywhere. I told the bus driver I was a prince. I told the mailman I could fly. I told my goldfish he was a dog. By Friday, I was almost as big as Chad. I made the team. But then I saw Benny outside, hugging a lamp post in the wind. I'd spent all week lying, while ignoring my best friend. So I told him the truth. Your drawing looks like a potato. I lost twenty pounds instantly. Benny said, it's fine, I'm not mad. And for the first time, he got twenty pounds heavier. The wind blew, and he didn't move an inch. So I told everyone the truth. I was not a prince. I could not fly. The goldfish was a fish. Coach kicked me off the team. Worth it. Then Benny asked, does it look like a potato? I stared at it. I said no. Some lies are worth the weight.
